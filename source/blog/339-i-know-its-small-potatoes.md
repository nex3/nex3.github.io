I know it's small potatoes in the scope of the illiteracy crisis as a whole, but
I'm seeing more and more people misuse "it's" and it's making me despair a bit.
I'm not even talking about in casual communication, I've seen it in written
works intended for public display, and it makes me so sad that people don't know
or don't care or both.

For reference: "its" without an apostrophe is possessive. "It wanted its food".
"It's" with an apostrophe is *only* correct as a shortening of "it is" or "it
has". "It's hungry". I know the rules of orthography are silly and arbitrary and
this one in particular is inconsistent with how other possessives are written,
but however arbitrary the rules are, following them still conveys that you the
writer care about your work and that you read other peoples'. Petty and
pointless[^1] rule violations suggest to the reader (and most strongly to
exactly those readers who do the most reading) that not only do you not take
pride in your work, but you don't engage with the wider art form you're trying
to contribute to enough to understand its basic patterns.

[^1]: There are definitely times when rules can be violated on purpose to make a
    point. Writing without capitals, especially in short-form text[^2], conveys
    a certain informality and serves as a tone marker. Leaving out apostrophes
    altogether does this even moreso, evoking pre-autocorrect T9 text messages.
    But an incorrect "it's" doesn't really communicate anything other than
    sloppiness on the writer's part.

[^2]: I've seen people do this in longer-form text as well but personally I find
    that it isn't worth the cost in poor legibility from lesser visual emphasis
    on sentence structure. Besides, a longer form work already conveys a degree
    of formality just from the mere fact that its length requires effort, so the
    pointed informality of lowercase can come off as pretentious.

Typos happen, and that's fine. I make them too! But when I'm writing something
substantial or making art to put out into the world, I get people to proofread
it so I can catch and fix them. You should too!
