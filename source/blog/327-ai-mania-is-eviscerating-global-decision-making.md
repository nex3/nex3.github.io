I don't love all of Suresh's framings around LLMs[^1], but I do appreciate that
his closer exposure to them has given him the perspective to write this piece.
This matches my experience very closely. I've so far managed to avoid
participating in the false zealotry he describes, but I'm not sure how much
longer I can manage it. My colleagues have not. I can hardly blame them.

Meanwhile, it's extremely clear to anyone who hasn't succumbed to the reality
distortion field that this monomania is completely corrosive to the
organization. Suresh documents the realities of this corrosion, although I
imagine most people reading this have seen it in one or another aspects of their
lives.

Personally, I'm just doing my best to grit my teeth and try to do good work as
best I can, both professionally and personally, until we get back to a point
where the managerial consensus is once again that "quality" is a real thing that
exists. Until then, I suffer.

[^1]: I feel that a hard-line zero-tolerance standpoint is necessary due to the
  myriad negative externalities and the strong tendency for "well I can see it
  being a bit useful for X" being taken in bad faith as a broad license to use
  it "where it makes sense". Even in cases where they're not strictly
  enforceable, zero tolerance policies establish a culture and enable
  enforcement of at least egregious offenses.
