  <p>I do agree that using these licenses is a good baseline, but I'm becoming
  more and more convinced that framing the entire struggle of liberatory
  computing primarily around licensing is a dead end. Licensing is necessary but
  not sufficient for user empowerment<sup><a
  href="https://technomancy.us/205#fn1">1</a></sup>. There's been plenty written
  on how the Free Software Foundation's purity spiral has done a great
  disservice to the users who are stuck with hardware that's not supported by
  free drivers or stuck on employer-mandated operating systems, and that's all
  absolutely true. But I'm starting to think that <em>freedom</em> is the wrong
  lens for this, and we should be focusing on <em>solidarity</em>. Solidarity
  encompasses freedom, but it doesn't ignore the fundamental interconnectedness
  of the struggle, and it doesn't glorify individualism.</p>
{% endgenericPost %}

Phil's writing here hits me hard, as someone who's trying to keep my head above
the rising tide of slop without having to *entirely* reinvent the entire
software ecosystem from first principles. I'm particularly interested in
"unslopped" forks of existing projects, which I think is a fecund area for
fighting back in a practical way—but one that requires a substantial amount of
effort. That said, if you've ever wanted to contribute to an important project
with tons of users, there's no better time than now to build an audience by
starting to maintain or help out with an unslopped fork!
