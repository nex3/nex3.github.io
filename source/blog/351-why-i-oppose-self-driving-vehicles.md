  <p>I find it very frustrating when cyclists speak well of Waymo! I don't
  believe these vehicles should be allowed on our streets in their current form,
  if ever, and I believe that we need to heavily legislate against tech company
  remote operation of vehicles generally.</p>

  <p>Like many things tech companies do, the scale at which they're able to do
  it requires us to treat them differently than private individuals or even
  smaller companies.</p>

  <p>The simplest example I can think of is "Waymo runs over a child." Usually,
  when a car strikes a kid, insurance companies and local police handle the
  incident - often very badly. Imagine, however, that someone with infinite
  wealth or power runs over a young person. In an incident where a car with
  <em>far more lawyers and wealth than any insurance company</em> strikes a
  child, I am sure that the family of that kid would not appreciate the power
  imbalance. Imagine having to go up against Google or Amazon to get justice for
  <em>vehicular violence?</em> In a country where killing someone with your car
  is already <em>basically legal?</em></p>

  <p>Long-term, there is little incentive for self-driving car companies to
  prioritize the safety of road users like cyclists. They can eat any amount of
  fine payments. They can bring any amount of legal influence to bear. It is
  simply not wise to allow this kind of power imbalance between road users! I
  think the threat of a tech company becoming a road user is, long-term, greater
  than the benefit of automatic driving.</p> <p>It does not matter if Waymos
  are, today, safer for cyclists than a regular driver. Long-term, they can do
  pretty much anything they want, and if they obtain any amount of regulatory
  capture when it comes to car usage, we're boned.</p>

  <p><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_of_Harry_Dunn">We pretty much
  know from the UK what can happen</a> when someone with a lot of power - and
  the ability to evade justice - kills someone on the road. Tech companies must
  be treated like state actors, like political actors - a tech company running
  over someone would be capable of all the same sneaky legal evasions as a
  national government. The police are already notorious for parking and driving
  irresponsibly - imagine what a tech company could accomplish, if they really
  wanted to? There is no barrier they cannot abuse except the barrier of a
  complete ban.</p>

  <p>A collision with a Waymo is something that may very well happen to me in
  the future, since I ride bikes on the road. Whenever I see a Waymo, I can only
  think, "Oh boy. That thing is covered in cameras and has the invisible power
  of infinite lawyers attached to it. I would not like to get into a conflict
  with the owners of that car! If it runs me over my family won't get any
  justice!"</p>

  <p>I think that long-term, the solution to traffic violence is not replacing
  cars with self-driving cars owned by powerful political actors, but rather
  removing them from the street environment entirely. If given the choice
  between "allowing the planet's most powerful and immoral entities to drive a
  car near me" and "not allowing cars to drive near me at all," I'd choose
  number 2. I support pedestrianization, or full separation of car traffic from
  other road users. I do not want a company with literally thousands of lawyers
  operating a vehicle near me <em>at all,</em> no matter how safe they seem to
  be in the year 2026.</p>
